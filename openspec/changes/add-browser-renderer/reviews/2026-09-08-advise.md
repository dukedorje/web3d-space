## Blind pass

1. Pin this SvelteKit repository as the browser renderer's sole product home so downstream work has one integration boundary.
2. Pin PlayCanvas as owner of mesh-and-splat rasterization because the existing app already creates its graphics device and depth-composites both forms.
3. Pin the continuous radiance field as source representation and Gaussian splats as the browser runtime cache, not interchangeable render architectures.
4. Pin an explicit v1 refusal of hardware ray tracing, browser path tracing, and in-browser NeRF MLP evaluation.
5. Pin a glTF-family world document that names imported meshes, splat references, transforms, camera, and lighting without inventing another scene graph.
6. Pin HDRI sky/environment data and distance-driven LOD as world-document concerns with deterministic degraded behavior.
7. Pin `$lib/gpu` as a compute sidecar whose separate `GPUDevice`, command submission, loss, and teardown lifecycle remain explicit.
8. Refuse an implied shared-device contract: current libraries do not expose safe external-device injection, and the existing stacks acquire devices independently.
9. Refuse a custom WGSL splat rasterizer or a parallel Threlte renderer unless a later change supplies new evidence and accepts replacement cost.
10. Accept the dual-device boundary as the key tradeoff: it avoids invasive engine coupling now, while spending memory and making synchronization/compositing less flexible.

## Comparison

The design and delta answer all ten concerns. The six architecture calls are explicit and mutually reinforcing: this repository is home; PlayCanvas owns mesh+splat rasterization; NeRF names the continuous authoring representation while 3DGS is the browser cache; hardware RT and per-frame path tracing are refused in v1; the world document is glTF-family with HDRI and distance LOD; and `$lib/gpu` remains a compute sidecar on its independently acquired device. The requirements turn those calls into observable scenarios instead of leaving them as preferences.

The device boundary is particularly well handled. The design acknowledges the two devices already present, refuses an unsupported external-device-injection contract, and assigns any later depth-buffer or compute interop to `add-gpu-exec` or `add-splat-cache`. That preserves the current PlayCanvas integration and avoids smuggling a custom splat renderer into this architecture-only change.

## Steelman against the blind take

My blind take asked for deterministic degraded behavior around HDRI and LOD. The submitted change is stronger at this scope by pinning the document vocabulary and the distance-culling invariant while leaving loader fallback mechanics to `add-world-model` and streaming mechanics to `add-splat-cache`. Specifying fallback behavior here would pre-design those nodes without implementation evidence.

The glTF-family wording also usefully permits either `KHR_gaussian_splatting` or sidecar caches. That keeps the world model stable if the extension or runtime packaging evolves, while still refusing a second scene graph. Likewise, calling 3DGS a cache of the field is an architectural data-flow constraint, not a claim that every Gaussian asset must be mechanically converted from a particular NeRF implementation.

## Tradeoff and disposition

The accepted cost is two independently owned GPU devices: higher resource pressure and less direct synchronization in exchange for avoiding a PlayCanvas fork or an unproven shared-device API. The change exposes that cost and places the interop decision in the correct later nodes. No host-engine, ownership, field-versus-cache, or v1 ray-tracing ambiguity remains, so the change is ready to act.
